Yes. ## Finding
Yes. Irreversibility has been formalized as a distinct category (i.e., a separate decision-relevant property, constraint, or penalty term) in both (a) decision-analytic/ethics-adjacent frameworks and (b) AI safety/value-alignment frameworks, though in many “ethical AI principles” documents it is treated more qualitatively than formally.

## Evidence in ethical decision-making / decision analysis
A clear formalization appears in precaution/option-value models where *irreversibility is represented structurally as a loss of future feasible options*. In a two-period model, the first-period decision determines a second-period opportunity set \(D(\alpha_1)\); a decision is defined as “more irreversible” when it yields a subset of feasible future choices (\(D(\alpha_1) \subseteq D(\alpha'_1)\)). This makes irreversibility a distinct category in the framework (separate from payoff magnitude) because it is encoded as a constraint on future action rather than merely an outcome cost, and it underpins an “option value of waiting” rationale for precaution under uncertainty. (gollier2003decisionmakingunderscientific pages 5-8)

## Evidence in AI value alignment / AI safety
### 1) Irreversibility as a distinct *side-effect* category via impact measures
In work on negative side effects/impact regularization, irreversibility is treated as a distinct property of transitions that can be directly targeted by the penalty definition. For example, reachability-based penalties (relative reachability) are explicitly discussed in terms of whether they penalize *irreversible transitions*; the undiscounted form is described as penalizing only irreversible transitions. This makes “irreversibility” a separable design target inside the metric, not just an informal concern. (krakovna2018penalizingsideeffects pages 11-13, krakovna2018penalizingsideeffects pages 3-5)
